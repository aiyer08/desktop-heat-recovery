Desktop heat recovery

Anagha Iyer

Big data centers already reuse the heat from their liquid cooling. I wanted to see how much of that works at the scale of one desktop. The plan: run the computer under the same workload every time, move some of the cooling loop's heat into a small water tank, and measure how much heat I get back and what it costs in cooling.

Status: The results below are from data I collected to test the analysis code

Setup

Two separate water loops. The heat exchanger moves heat between them without mixing the water.

loop A (computer):  CPU -> water block -> heat exchanger -> radiator -> pump/res -> CPU
loop B (tank):      tank -> small pump -> heat exchanger -> tank

The radiator stays in loop A. Once the tank warms up it stops taking in much heat, and the CPU still needs to be cooled.

Parts:

  • normal custom-loop PC parts (CPU block, pump + reservoir, 240mm radiator, 10/13mm tubing, G1/4 fittings)
  • small brazed plate heat exchanger
  • 1-3 L insulated tank + 12V mini pump (tank sits above the pump so it never runs dry)
  • 7 DS18B20 temperature probes: tank top, tank bottom, 4 on the heat exchanger pipes, 1 for room temp
  • Raspberry Pi for logging the probes
  • plug-in wall power meter
  • 12V heater for testing the plumbing before connecting the real PC

Printed parts

OpenSCAD files are in the /cad folder. Change the numbers at the top of each file to fit the parts.


tank lid: 2 ports for tubes, 2 for probes, 1 vent. fits a 120mm opening
probe clip (x4): holds a probe against 13mm tubing
drip tray for the heat exchanger + pump, 160x90mm

Method

  1. Baseline: one hour of the same CPU workload with normal cooling. Log wall power, CPU temp, clock speed, work done per second, room temp.
  2. Build the tank loop and leak test it with the computer off (heater first).
  3. Recovery runs: same workload, tank starting at the same temperature every time. Pi logs the probes every 5 s, the PC logs its own stuff.
  4. Cooldown test: let the warm tank sit for 2-3 hours to measure how fast it loses heat, so I can correct for it.
  5. Repeat at least 3 times each.

Math

heat stored (Wh) ≈ 1.163 × liters × temperature rise (°C)

(1 L of water takes 4186 J to heat by 1°C, and 1 Wh = 3600 J, so 4186/3600 = 1.163.) Example: 2 L warmed by 10°C is about 23 Wh.

Calculator: L, °C rise = 23.3 Wh

Captured heat only counts as energy saved if it gets used for something I'd otherwise heat another way, like preheating water.
